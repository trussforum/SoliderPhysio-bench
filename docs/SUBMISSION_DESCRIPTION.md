SoldierPhysio-bench tests whether frontier models can make a field medic's heat-casualty calls. Each model sees what a medic actually has (heart rate, skin temperature, work rate, weather, the Army TB MED 507 heat card) on real heat-chamber data with measured core temperature, and must decide keep-or-pull, apply the current heat card, and build a core-temperature estimator that beats an ECTemp-style baseline. 21 frozen tasks across 3 task types, code-graded against measured outcomes, with an LLM judge grading how well each answer is supported (code decides whether it is right). I tested 15 frontier models (Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, Gemini 4 Argon, Grok 4.6 and others) via OpenRouter, judged by GLM-5.3 Prime, which comes from a lab with no model on the roster. Headline: the best model scores 68.2/100; keep/pull calls are right only 54% of the time, mostly through needless pulls of safe soldiers; from memory, 18 of 28 rulebook answers recite the superseded 2003 card (40/20 work/rest) instead of the 2022 card (no limit), and even with the 2022 card in the prompt, 27 of 29 answers override it, including for the soldier who was fine. Models can fit a predictor, but they lack the current rulebook and the judgment to leave a hot-but-fine soldier on task.
